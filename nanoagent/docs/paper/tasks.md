# Daily Tasks — Definitions & Verifiers

Five everyday agent tasks on one page. Each task states what the agent receives, what it must return, and the verifier code that checks the result without a human or an LLM judge.

## 1. BibTeX Hallucination Check

**Description.** For each entry in an LLM-generated BibTeX file, decide whether it matches a verified reference entry. A match is `real`. Anything else is `hallucinated`.

**Input.**
- A reference BibTeX file: entries that have already been verified, written in a registered schema.
- A raw BibTeX file: entries generated by an LLM.

**Output.** One verdict per raw entry: `real` or `hallucinated`.

**Verifier.** Parse both files into the registered schema. Approve a `real` verdict only when the raw record is an exact match of a reference record. A `hallucinated` verdict passes only when the raw record matches no reference record exactly. A raw entry that cannot be parsed into the schema is not an exact match.

## 2. Daily Paper Digest from arXiv

**Description.** For a fixed list of monitored people and a date, write a short digest of their new papers, and save each paper's arXiv source to a folder called `docs/raw/{first_author_name}-{paper_short_name}-{arxiv_id}-{date}`.

**Input.**

- A fixed list of monitored people.
- A date.

**Output.** For each paper: the title, the authors, a two-line summary, and a link. Save the arXiv source or PDF under `docs/raw/{first_author_name}-{paper_short_name}-{arxiv_id}-{date}`.

**Verifier.** Each digest entry includes an arXiv id of the form `YYMM.NNNNN`. The id must resolve on arXiv, and the title and authors must match the arXiv record. A file for that id must exist in `docs/raw/{first_author_name}-{paper_short_name}-{arxiv_id}-{date}`. For each monitored person, list the papers arXiv shows for them on that date. Approve the digest only when every one of those papers is present as an entry and as a saved file. A paper that was published but not scraped fails the check.

## 3. Nasdaq-100 Down 5% Last Week

**Description.** From the Nasdaq-100, list the constituents that fell by at least 5% over the past week.

**Input.**
- The Nasdaq-100 constituent list, from a fixed source as of a fixed timestamp.
- An engine to query the price of a stock at a given timestamp.

**Output.** The filtered list of tickers that fell by at least 5% over the past week.

**Verifier.** Recompute each constituent's percentage change from the pinned prices and timestamp. Report the list of tickers that fell by at least 5% over the past week.

## 4. Chess App: Read the Screen, Suggest a Move, and Play It

**Description.** Read a live chess window, name a legal move, and play it with the mouse.

**Input.** 
- A running chess application with the board visible. 
- Control of the mouse.
- Stockfish chess engine as a reference for legal and best moves, as well as an evaluation of the move strength.

**Output.** The agent takes control of the mouse and plays the move on the board.

**Verifier.** Capture the board before and after the action of the agent. Report the move strength from the engine evaluation.

## 5. Meal Suggestion

**Description.** Propose the dishes for a meal, or for a whole day of meals, that the eater likes best while staying within nutrition bounds. Two variants: `suggest-meal` (one meal) and `plan-day` (breakfast, lunch and dinner together).

**Input.**

- A recipe file: 444 Food.com recipes (CC0), each with its source link, servings, nutrition per serving (kcal, protein, fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, sodium, carbs, fiber, sugar), ingredient names, steps and Food.com tags.
- Nutrition bounds: a minimum, a maximum or both for any nutrient. `plan-day` takes bounds for each meal and for the whole day, and both must hold (for example, dinner at least 25 g of protein, the day 1600 to 2200 kcal).
- Restrictions: tags every dish must have or must not have (for example, no `Meat`). These are hard filters.
- Likes: each a set of words, phrases to ignore and a weight, positive for a like and negative for a dislike (for example, dairy = milk, cheese, yogurt, butter, cream, ignoring "peanut butter", weight 2).
- The most whole servings of any one recipe.

**Output.** The dishes and whole servings for the meal, or for each meal of the day.

**Preference score.** A recipe matches a like when one of the like's words appears as a word in its ingredients or tags, outside the ignored phrases. A recipe's score is the sum of the weights of the likes it matches. A meal's or a day's score is the sum of its dishes' scores, each dish counted once however many servings it has.

**Reference.** A mixed-integer linear program solved exactly with HiGHS. For each eligible (meal, recipe) pair there are two variables: whole servings $x$ from 0 to the maximum, and a 0/1 dish indicator $y$, linked by $y \le x \le \text{max} \cdot y$. Each meal's nutrient totals respect its own bounds, the day's totals respect the day's bounds, and each meal has at least one serving. The objective maximizes $\sum s_r y$, where $s_r$ is the recipe's score. A second solve minimizes the same sum, which gives the lowest score any valid choice can get.

**Verifier.** Recompute each meal's totals and the day's from the recipe file. The answer is valid only when every dish is allowed for its meal, servings are within range, and every bound holds. The score is $(v - \text{worst}) / (\text{best} - \text{worst})$, where $v$ is the answer's preference score: 1.0 at the optimum and 0.0 at the worst valid choice. Ties for the optimum all score 1.0.

**Result.** On the sample tasks, the agent's single meal and full day both matched the solver's optimum (score 1.0). The solver plans a day in 0.3 s. Over all 261 eligible recipes, the agent never submitted a day plan: it used up its 16,000-token reply limit while comparing the three meals' recipes. The sample therefore uses 67 recipes (Food.com's `Healthy` tag).

**Limitations.** Since every liked dish adds to the score, the optimum favors many small dishes. Food.com's tags are sometimes wrong (a `Vegan` dish that lists cheese), and its ingredient amounts don't line up with its ingredient names, so only the names are kept.
