# Classic World Models

### World Models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018)

The foundational work by Ha and Schmidhuber [@ha2018world] introduced the modern world model paradigm. Their architecture consists of three components: a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) that compresses observations into a latent space, a Mixture Density Network-RNN (MDN-RNN) that models the dynamics in latent space, and a compact controller that operates on the latent representation. The agent is trained entirely in "dreams" -- rollouts generated by the learned model -- enabling rapid policy learning with minimal real-world interaction. They demonstrated this on the VizDoom and CarRacing environments.

### PlaNet

Hafner et al. (2019) [@hafner2019learning] introduced PlaNet (Deep Planning Network), which learns a latent dynamics model and plans online using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), which combines deterministic and stochastic state components to capture both predictable dynamics and environmental uncertainty. PlaNet achieved competitive performance on continuous control tasks from image observations using far fewer environment interactions than model-free methods.

### Dreamer (v1, v2, v3)

The Dreamer series by Hafner et al. represents the most successful line of latent world model research:

**Dreamer v1 (2020)** [@hafner2020dream] extended PlaNet by learning a policy and value function within the imagined latent trajectories, using backpropagation through the dynamics model. This "imagination-based" policy learning proved more efficient than CEM planning.

**Dreamer v2 (2021)** [@hafner2021mastering] introduced discrete latent representations using categorical variables, which improved stability and performance. It was the first model-based agent to achieve human-level performance on the Atari 100k benchmark.

**Dreamer v3 (2023)** [@hafner2023mastering] achieved a major milestone: a single, fixed set of hyperparameters that masters diverse domains including continuous control, Atari games, Minecraft (crafting a diamond), and DMLab navigation. Key innovations include symlog predictions (handling reward scales across orders of magnitude), free bits for KL balancing, and improved discrete world model representations. DreamerV3 demonstrated that world models can generalize across fundamentally different domain types without domain-specific tuning.
